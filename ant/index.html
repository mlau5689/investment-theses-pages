01

The thesis in one page

Anthropic is the closed model to watch, and the hedge to price, not the compounder to buy.

Nov 22 addendum, thesis build: the Anthropic thesis is created as a watch-first monitor and a conditional hedge leg of the totality thesis. Verdict WATCH; the 1-of-1 verdict is FAILS. Anthropic builds Claude, the strongest coding and agentic model family, and it went public on November 19, 2026 in the largest IPO on record by proceeds at pricing: $81.34B raised in the base offering at $83 a share, with a concurrent $26B private placement from its own biggest customers and suppliers. The company grew revenue 1,127% year over year in Q3 2026 and holds about $146.6B in cash. It also carries about $900B in commitments of which only $13.8B is non-cancelable, pays 55-58% of its revenue for compute, and has never produced a sustained operating profit. The question this thesis answers is narrow: should Anthropic be owned as a hedge to the whole system, the get-out-of-jail-free card if the black hole grows too powerful to stop? The answer is conditional: not at $88.91, about 15x the $81B run rate. At about $60 a share (10x sales) or about $30 (5x), with the evidence conditions holding, a hedge-sized position is in scope. Until then, watch it, because what happens to Anthropic is material to the SpaceX thesis even with no position: Anthropic is SpaceX's biggest compute customer. Read the totality thesis.

02

The hedge case: for owning

If the black hole wins, owning a piece of it is the only hedge that pays.

Capture is the business. The black-hole thesis is that customers adopt closed models out of fear of missing general intelligence, and the price they pay is not just dollars but the alpha that makes their company competitive. The model provider learns that alpha, productizes it, and seeks to replace the customer. If that dynamic runs to its end state, the provider becomes the one company that provides everything, and the only position that pays in that world is a piece of the provider.

Coexistence is real. Founders Fund holds both Anthropic and Palantir. The hedge is against where value lands, not against AI demand itself. The model layer and the deployment layer are complements, not substitutes: Palantir is model-agnostic and builds on open or closed models. Holding both is a bet that value lands somewhere in the stack, not a bet against the stack.

The coding lead is real. Claude Code is used by 65.5% of agent users in the Stack Overflow 2026 Developer Survey, up from 10% in 2025, against GitHub Copilot at 58.7% and OpenAI Codex at 29.5%. On SWE-bench Verified, Claude Opus 4.5 scores 80.9% against GPT-5.1-Codex-Max at 77.9% (third-party tracker). Coding is the wedge where a model becomes infrastructure: developers build workflows around it, and workflows are sticky.

The balance sheet is a fortress. About $146.6B in cash at September 30, 2026, before IPO proceeds, plus a $26B concurrent private placement funded by Google, Nvidia, AMD, Broadcom, Micron, Microsoft, and TSMC. The suppliers are funding their biggest customer, which is both a strength (the money is there) and a warning (the money has a motive).

Growth is extraordinary. Revenue of $41M in 2023, $386M in 2024, $4.59B in 2025, $16.29B in the first half of 2026, and $16.96B in Q3 2026, up 1,127% year over year. The run rate is about $81B as of October 31, 2026. Net revenue retention is about 900%: customers who adopt, expand.

SpaceX benefits either way. Anthropic depends on SpaceX for compute, and SpaceX trains its own closed-source model, Grok. If Anthropic wins, SpaceX collects the rent. If Grok catches up, SpaceX owns the model. The dependency is leverage, and the totality thesis holds the landlord.

03

The fail case: against owning

The model is not 10x better. The moat depends on customers not waking up.

The capability gap has collapsed. The MMLU gap between the best open and best proprietary models went from 17.5 points at the end of 2023 to 0.3 points in early 2026. DeepSeek V4 Flash is priced about 89x cheaper than Claude Opus 4.8 on output tokens ($0.28 vs $25.00 per million tokens). A model that is not 10x better than its peers cannot earn a compounder verdict under the 1-of-1 doctrine, and the open models keep closing the gap on the hard tasks too.

The moat depends on customers not waking up. The black-hole moat is exactly what the sovereignty thesis bets against: customers waking up to the price they are paying in alpha and pivoting to sovereign systems in time. The hedge pays only if the totality thesis is wrong. That is the definition of insurance, not a compounder leg, and insurance is priced accordingly.

The commitments are mostly terminable. Total commitments are about $900.9B at September 30, 2026, but only $13.8B of revenue is under non-cancelable contracts. About $70.7B is due within 12 months, about 86% of the run rate, and about $186.2B is terminable or conditional. A $900B number that can shrink on 90 days' notice is a pipeline, not a backlog.

Compute eats the revenue. Compute is 55-58% of revenue, and the suppliers who provide it also funded the private placement. Sequential revenue growth fell from 147% to 45%: the tokenmaxxing peak has passed, and the business is now growing into its cost structure rather than outrunning it.

Operating profit is in fits and starts. GAAP operating income was +$974M in Q2 FY2026 and -$567M in Q3 FY2026. The nine-month net loss of $104B is mostly non-cash fair-value changes on convertible notes and SAFEs (about $72.4B in the first half), but the operating line itself has not sustained a profit, and there is no earnings floor under the multiple.

The IPO was a distribution. The founders sold 23.6M secondary shares, and Series H holders were marked up about 45% in six months at the $1.4T valuation. The IPO raised a massive balance sheet while private holders sold into the public market, which is the same distribution incentive structure as any large offering: the people closest to the business took the opportunity to sell.

A black hole that eats its customers eventually eats its own revenue base. If the model provider replaces its customers, it must replace them fast enough to keep growing, because every customer it consumes is a revenue line that ends. The end state they seek, one company providing everything, is also the end state where the customer base that funds them no longer exists.

04

The funnel: data is the binding constraint

Model growth is not only about the ability to train. At some point the binding constraint becomes the information the model can capture.

Compute has diminishing returns. Past a point, more training flops buy less capability, and the differentiator becomes data quality and access. Model quality is a measure of the quality of information the model has access to. The black hole grows by what funnels into it, and it is only as strong as what funnels into it.

The social funnel. X's real-time human discourse is a direct link to how humanity thinks and what is going on in the world, and it is funneled into Grok. X restricts who can train on that data, keeping it for Grok. That is Grok's data advantage and part of the SpaceX hedge: the only live, proprietary feed of human thought at planetary scale.

The enterprise funnel. Customers feed the design and nature of their business into the model: prompts, workflows, usage patterns, evaluations. State the mechanism honestly: the funnel works even where the provider does not literally train on customer data. Anthropic's API terms say it does not train on customer API data by default. The provider still learns the demand, the workflows, and what to build, and productizes it for everyone. The learning is in the product roadmap, not only in the weights.

The self-limiting property. The funnel is both the growth engine and the fragility. If customers stop feeding it, the funnel dries up and the black hole starves, which is exactly the "cut off their growth faster than they can pay their debts" dynamic: Anthropic's commitments come due on a schedule, and the revenue that services them depends on customers who are deciding, right now, whether to keep feeding the funnel or pivot to sovereign systems.

The counter-moves. The labs use synthetic data, reinforcement learning, and test-time compute to mitigate the data wall. These mitigate it; they do not replace proprietary real-world information. Epoch AI and others estimate that public text data is exhausted in the 2026-2028 window (estimates, not fact), which is why the proprietary funnels matter more every year, and why starving them matters more too.

05

The dependency: Anthropic and SpaceX

Anthropic is SpaceX's biggest compute customer. The dependency runs both ways.

Anthropic accounts for about one third of SpaceX's Q3 2026 revenue. The compute rent is about $1.25B a month, rising to about $3.1B a month at full delivery expected in December 2026. The contracts allow either side to terminate on 90 days' notice after the initial non-cancelable periods end in January and March 2027. That makes Anthropic material to the SpaceX thesis even with no Anthropic position: if Anthropic's business falters, SpaceX's largest revenue line is at risk at the exact moment its GPU-debt service begins; if Anthropic thrives, SpaceX collects the rent and Grok closes the gap from inside the same building.

The leverage is SpaceX's. Grok has not yet caught up to Anthropic or OpenAI capability, but it is close, and the dependency lets SpaceX force the others to bend as Grok closes the gap. A customer that can build its own model is not a captive customer. Watch the renewal windows: January and March 2027 are when the non-cancelable periods end and the 90-day termination clock starts. The totality thesis carries the same dependency as a core thread.

06

The floor: cash and contracts, not earnings

There is no earnings floor. The floor is cash and contracted revenue, and the contracted part is thin.

Cash is about $146.6B at September 30, 2026, before IPO proceeds, and the $26B concurrent private placement adds to it. That is a real floor: the company can fund years of compute and losses from the balance sheet. But the contracted floor is only $13.8B of non-cancelable revenue against about $900B in total commitments, and the operating line has not sustained a profit. A growth disappointment reprices the multiple with no earnings floor underneath. The floor is honest, and it is not a compounder's floor.

Floor breakers: the run rate stalling while commitments come due; a Grok or open-model release that collapses the coding premium; a sovereignty pivot among large enterprise customers that starves the funnel; the SpaceX compute contracts renegotiated or terminated on 90 days' notice.

07

The hedge zone: priced, conditional, untriggered

The hedge is priced on sales, not on a compounder multiple, and the trigger is conditional on evidence, not on price alone.

The derivation starts from the $81B run rate as of October 31, 2026 and the shares outstanding after the offering, about 13.22B (Class A 7,583,103,996 plus Class N 5,636,538,278, per the final prospectus). Ten times the run rate is $810B, divided by 13.22B shares, about $61 a share. Five times is $405B, about $31 a share. Rounded: about $60 at 10x sales, about $30 at 5x sales. On a fully diluted basis (about 16.9-18.1B shares including options, RSUs, and convertible notes), the same levels are about $45 and $22.50. At the November 20 close of $88.91, the market value on shares outstanding is about $1.19T, about 15x the run rate. The trigger is untriggered.

Price alone does not trigger the hedge. At or below a trigger level, at least one evidence condition must also hold: the non-cancelable share of commitments rising, sustained operating profitability, or durable capture evidence. If owned at all, it is insurance-sized, never a compounder leg. The hedge pays only if the totality thesis is wrong, and insurance is bought at insurance prices.

08

Needle milestones

The small set of milestones that move the measured odds of this thesis the most.

  • Sustained operating profitability (success): two consecutive quarters of positive GAAP operating income. Down: losses deepen as the $900B commitments come due.
  • Non-cancelable share of commitments rising (success): revenue under non-cancelable contracts materially above $13.8B and rising as a share of the $900B total. Down: commitments shrink, or stay terminable while the run rate stalls.
  • Grok capability parity (risk-mitigation): Grok matches Claude on independent benchmarks, weakening Anthropic's leverage over SpaceX. Down: Claude extends its lead and the dependency deepens.
  • Anthropic-SpaceX dependency renewal or break (risk-mitigation): compute contracts renewed or extended past the Jan/Mar 2027 non-cancelable windows on stable terms. Down: either side gives 90 days' notice, or terms reset sharply against SpaceX.
  • Durable capture evidence (growth): retention and expansion show durable lock-in, net revenue retention holds and enterprise expansion continues. Down: churn rises or sovereignty pivots accelerate among large customers.
  • Token-demand re-acceleration vs the tokenmaxxing peak (growth): sequential revenue growth re-accelerates above the 45% print. Down: sequential growth keeps falling as the tokenmaxxing peak recedes.

09

Verdict: watch, and what would change it

WATCH. The 1-of-1 verdict is FAILS.

Anthropic is not a 1-of-1: the model is not 10x better than its peers, the capability gap has collapsed, and the moat depends on customers not waking up. It is not a compounder, and it earns no compounder probability or price zone. It is a watch thesis with a conditional hedge leg. What would change the verdict: sustained operating profitability plus a rising non-cancelable share of commitments would make the floor real; durable capture evidence would make the moat real; and a price at the trigger levels with that evidence would make the hedge worth buying at insurance size. Until then, the right posture is to watch Anthropic closely, because its fate is SpaceX's largest revenue line and the totality thesis's sharpest test.

What to watch

The open questions, tracked through this thesis and its siblings.

  • Does the black hole win, or do customers wake up and pivot to sovereign systems before the alpha is given away?
  • Does the non-cancelable share of the $900B commitments rise, or do the commitments stay terminable and the run rate prove fragile?
  • Does Anthropic reach sustained operating profitability, or do compute costs keep it in fits and starts?
  • Does Grok close the capability gap before Anthropic's SpaceX compute contracts come up for renewal in early 2027?
  • Does the data funnel keep feeding the model, or does the sovereignty pivot starve it faster than Anthropic can pay its debts?
  • At what price does the hedge make sense, and does the evidence at that price support owning insurance rather than a compounder?
  • What happens to SpaceX's revenue if Anthropic's compute contracts are renegotiated or terminated on 90 days' notice?

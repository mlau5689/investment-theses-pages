01

The thesis in one page

It would be naive to watch Anthropic and not OpenAI. This thesis completes the closed-source monitor set.

Nov 22 addendum, thesis build: the OpenAI thesis is created as a pre-IPO watch thesis. Verdict WATCH; the 1-of-1 verdict is FAILS. No ticker, no zones; the IPO itself is the needle milestone. From the closed-source lens the system monitors three things: Grok, inside the SpaceX thesis; Anthropic, in the Anthropic thesis; and OpenAI, here. OpenAI is the largest consumer AI surface on earth (ChatGPT at 1.2B+ weekly users) and the deepest compute debtor in the industry (about $1.4T in commitments over about eight years). It filed a confidential S-1 on June 8, 2026, and the base case is an IPO in 2027. Q3 2026 revenue was about $10B, the first $10B quarter, against an adjusted operating loss of $10.9B, and the company does not expect profitability until 2029-2030. The black-hole test is the same as for Anthropic, applied to a different funnel: distribution at consumer scale, plus enterprise workflows. The verdict is the same too, with a different open question: the distribution moat is the candidate that could make OpenAI a 1-of-1, and it is unproven. Read the totality thesis.

02

The funnel: consumer distribution and enterprise workflows

Data, not compute, is the binding constraint. OpenAI's funnels are the widest in the industry, and the most exposed.

Model growth is not only about the ability to train. Past a point, more compute buys less capability, and the differentiator becomes data quality and access. The black hole grows by what funnels into it, and it is only as strong as what funnels into it.

The consumer funnel. ChatGPT is the largest consumer AI surface: 800M+ weekly users in December 2025, 900M+ in February 2026, 1B+ in August 2026, 1.2B+ at DevDay on September 29, 2026, with GPT-6 launching October 7. Every conversation is a signal about what people want, how they ask, and what works. That is a funnel no competitor can replicate without the distribution, and it is also the funnel most exposed to a consumer backlash: the moment users feel their conversations are the product, the funnel narrows.

The enterprise funnel. 2.5M enterprise customers, plus ChatGPT Work and Codex at 35M weekly active users. The mechanism is the same as Anthropic's, stated honestly: the funnel works even where the provider does not literally train on customer data. The provider still learns the demand, the workflows, and what to build, and productizes it for everyone. The learning is in the product roadmap, not only in the weights.

Compare and contrast. Anthropic's funnel is enterprise-first and coding-led; Grok's is the X social funnel, a direct link to how humanity thinks in real time. OpenAI's is consumer-first at a scale neither of the others has, plus an enterprise business growing underneath it. The consumer funnel is wider and shallower; the enterprise funnel is narrower and deeper. The question is which one feeds the model better, and which one survives the sovereignty pivot: consumers have less to protect than enterprises, but they also have less reason to stay.

The self-limiting property. The funnel is both the growth engine and the fragility. If customers stop feeding it, the funnel dries up and the black hole starves, which is exactly the "cut off their growth faster than they can pay their debts" dynamic: OpenAI's $1.4T in compute commitments comes due on a schedule, and the revenue that services it depends on users who are deciding, right now, whether to keep feeding the funnel or move to sovereign systems. Synthetic data, reinforcement learning, and test-time compute mitigate the data wall; they do not replace proprietary real-world information. Epoch AI and others estimate public text data is exhausted in the 2026-2028 window (estimates, not fact).

03

The coding question

Anthropic has been winning in coding. Most developers turn to Claude models for coding. What would OpenAI need to take it back?

The evidence is consistent across surveys and benchmarks. In the Stack Overflow 2026 Developer Survey, Claude Code is used by 65.5% of agent users (8,030 respondents), up from 10% in 2025; GitHub Copilot is at 58.7% and OpenAI Codex at 29.5%. On SWE-bench Verified, Claude Opus 4.5 scores 80.9% against GPT-5.1-Codex-Max at 77.9% (third-party tracker). The counter-evidence: on Terminal-Bench 2.0, Codex scores 77.3% against Claude Code at 65.4%, and vendor-reported figures put Codex CLI with GPT-5.5 at 88.7% against Claude Code with Opus 4.7 at 87.6% (treat vendor-reported figures cautiously; they are not independent).

What OpenAI needs to take coding back: a Codex release that wins SWE-bench Verified outright, not just on a favorable bench; developer adoption that shows up in the next Stack Overflow survey above Claude Code; and an agentic workflow that developers choose when they are spending their own money, not when it is bundled. Coding is the wedge where a model becomes infrastructure, and infrastructure is sticky. Until Codex leads on the independent measures, the coding premium belongs to Anthropic, and OpenAI's distribution advantage does not extend to the workflow where developers live.

04

The dependency: who OpenAI depends on, and who depends on OpenAI

SpaceX sells zero to OpenAI. OpenAI's compute comes from elsewhere, and the bill is the largest in the industry.

Who OpenAI depends on. Microsoft and Azure first: an additional $250B in services from October 2025, about 72% of OpenAI's compute in Microsoft and Oracle halls today (estimate), and Microsoft recorded $10.0B in revenue from OpenAI commercial arrangements in the quarter to September 30, 2026. Oracle and Stargate second: about $300B over about five years, Abilene at 618MW, inside the $500B Stargate program with SoftBank and MGX. Then AWS ($38B over 7 years, November 2025), CoreWeave (about $22.4B cumulative), Google Cloud (deal October 21, 2026, frontier models via Model Garden, Codex workflows coming; CoreWeave provides some of the capacity to Google for OpenAI), and Cerebras ($10B, January 2026). Broadcom custom silicon is reported at $350B and 10 GW of deployment. Total commitments: about $1.4T over about eight years. OpenAI ended 2025 with 1.9 GW of compute and has about 3.2 GW today.

Who depends on OpenAI. Microsoft's $10.0B quarterly revenue from the relationship is the largest single dependency, and it cuts both ways: Microsoft holds about 27% of OpenAI (about $135B; about 24% as of September 30, 2026 per its 10-Q), with rights to the models until 2032, commercial rights post-AGI through 2032, and a 20% revenue share. The April 2026 amendment removed Microsoft's right of first refusal on new cloud business, and OpenAI can serve products on any cloud, which is why the Google Cloud deal exists. The suppliers who fund OpenAI's compute are also its largest revenue line and its largest shareholders: the same distribution incentive structure as Anthropic's IPO, where the suppliers fund their biggest customer.

SpaceX sells zero. Musk said on November 17, 2026 that SpaceX sells zero to OpenAI. OpenAI's direct model supply to SpaceX-owned Cursor was set to end on November 12, 2026, on notice given August 28. Model access is now competitive leverage: the closed providers restrict each other's access to their models, and the restriction runs against SpaceX's own products. That is the model war in miniature, and it is why Grok's independence matters to the totality thesis.

05

The black-hole verdict

The same 1-of-1 doctrine, applied to the largest consumer AI surface.

In the model: no. The same capability-gap collapse as Anthropic. The MMLU gap between the best open and best proprietary models went from 17.5 points at the end of 2023 to 0.3 points in early 2026, and open models keep closing the gap on the hard tasks. GPT is not 10x better than its peers, and no model-only moat survives that collapse.

In distribution: the candidate moat, unproven. ChatGPT at 1.2B+ weekly users is the largest consumer AI surface, and distribution at that scale is the one candidate for a durable advantage. Test it honestly: distribution is a moat only if it survives the coding gap (developers are the most valuable users, and they are choosing Claude), the ads pivot (advertising reached a $1B annualized run rate in under 200 days, and ads change what the product optimizes for), and the sovereignty pivot (consumers have less to protect, but they also have less reason to stay). None of those tests has been passed yet.

In the black hole: customer capture, unproven. The capture mechanism is the same as Anthropic's: customers adopt out of FOMO on general intelligence and pay in alpha, not just dollars. OpenAI's capture is earlier and shallower than Anthropic's enterprise capture, because its funnel is consumer-first. The conditions that would change the verdict: Codex taking the coding lead on independent measures; weekly users holding above 1.2B with enterprise customers growing past 2.5M; and adjusted operating losses narrowing for two consecutive quarters toward the 2029-2030 profitability target.

06

The IPO: a second black hole going public

OpenAI plans to IPO, following the same narrative as Anthropic. The base case is 2027.

The filing chain, dated: a confidential S-1 was filed June 8, 2026 with Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley, with an original target of September 2026. Altman said on September 12, 2026 (Fortune) that it would not be 2026. CFO Sarah Friar said on August 19, 2026 that OpenAI will be a public company in 2027, or sooner if the business inflects. The base case is 2027. The restructuring completed October 28, 2025: OpenAI Group PBC, with the OpenAI Foundation (nonprofit) holding 26% (about $130B) and controlling the board, Microsoft about 27%, and employees and investors 47%.

What an OpenAI IPO means for the totality thesis. A second black hole going public, with the same distribution incentives as Anthropic's IPO: founders and insiders selling, suppliers funding their biggest customer, and a massive balance sheet raised while private holders sell into the public market. The valuation to watch is the private mark: $852B post-money at March 31, 2026 ($122B round, the largest private round in history), and a bridge round first close of about $11B at a reported $1.25T valuation on November 6, 2026 (the round was cut from $30B at about $1.4T to about $20B). If the IPO prices below the bridge mark, the repricing is itself the signal: the market is discounting the $1.4T compute bill against a 2029-2030 profitability target.

Would the Anthropic hedge logic apply at its IPO price? In principle, yes: the same derivation (a multiple of the run rate on shares outstanding, with a fully-diluted sensitivity) would price the hedge, and the same evidence conditions (sustained operating profitability, a rising non-cancelable share of commitments, durable capture evidence) would gate it. In practice, the IPO price is the first real test, and the test has not happened yet. Until it does, the OpenAI thesis is a monitor, not a hedge: there is nothing to buy, and nothing to price.

07

The floor

Private cash and Microsoft backing, not earnings.

OpenAI is private, so there is no public balance sheet to floor against. The observable floor is the cash raised (the $122B round closed March 31, 2026 at an $852B post-money valuation, plus a bridge round first close of about $11B at a reported $1.25T valuation on November 6, 2026) and the Microsoft relationship, which is also a dependency. The company does not expect profitability until 2029-2030 and projects a $14B loss in 2026. A growth disappointment reprices the private valuation with no earnings floor underneath, and the $1.4T in compute commitments over about eight years is a fixed cost against that.

Floor breakers: the bridge round repricing down sharply; a compute supplier cutting capacity or tightening terms; ChatGPT weekly users stalling or falling; the IPO slipping past 2027.

08

Needle milestones

The small set of milestones that move the measured odds of this thesis the most.

  • The IPO (success): OpenAI files publicly and prices an IPO, on terms that let the hedge logic be tested at a real price. Down: the IPO slips past 2027, or the bridge round reprices down sharply.
  • Coding recapture (growth): Codex takes the lead in developer adoption or on SWE-bench Verified. Down: Claude Code extends its lead and Codex stays a distant third.
  • Path to profitability (success): adjusted operating losses narrow for two consecutive quarters toward the 2029-2030 profitability target. Down: operating losses widen past the $14B projected 2026 loss with no narrowing path.
  • Compute commitments vs revenue (risk-mitigation): revenue growth re-accelerates toward the commitment schedule, or commitments are renegotiated down. Down: commitments keep growing while revenue growth decelerates, widening the fixed-cost gap.
  • ChatGPT distribution durability (growth): weekly users hold above 1.2B with enterprise customers growing past 2.5M. Down: weekly users stall or fall, or ads cannibalize the subscription base.
  • Dependency shifts (risk-mitigation): a second or third cloud becomes a material share of compute, reducing single-supplier dependence. Down: Microsoft or Oracle terms tighten, or a supplier cuts capacity.

09

Verdict: watch, and what would change it

WATCH. The 1-of-1 verdict is FAILS.

OpenAI is not a 1-of-1: the model is not 10x better, the distribution moat is unproven against the coding gap and the compute bill, and the capture is unproven. It is not a compounder, and it earns no compounder probability or price zone, because there is nothing to buy yet. What would change the verdict: the IPO itself, which turns a private mark into a public price and lets the hedge logic be tested; Codex taking the coding lead on independent measures; and a narrowing path to the 2029-2030 profitability target. Until then, the right posture is to watch OpenAI as the third closed-source monitor, because its distribution is the largest in the industry and its compute bill is the largest too.

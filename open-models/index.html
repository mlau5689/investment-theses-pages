01

The thesis in one page

The open-source monitor for the whole model scene. Five standing questions, answered as a live series.

Nov 22 addendum, thesis build: the open-models thesis is created as the open-source monitor. Verdict: closing on capability, widening on time. A landscape thesis, not a position. The closed monitors are Grok, inside the SpaceX thesis; Anthropic, in the Anthropic thesis; and OpenAI, in the OpenAI thesis. This thesis is the open-source monitor, and it answers five standing questions: how are open-source models evolving? Are they closing the gap on closed, or is the gap widening? How are things changing? How are the black holes growing? Have they reached a point of no return? The scope is all model types, not just language models: voice-only, image-only, video, and emerging types. The short answer: the benchmark gaps have effectively closed, the price gap is enormous (DeepSeek V4 Flash is about 89x cheaper than Claude Opus 4.8 on output tokens), and the gap persists on the hardest agentic tasks, where the average time lag grew from 3 months to 4 months. Read the totality thesis.

02

The gap, measured

The thesis tracks the gap as a live series, not a single snapshot. Four measures, each dated.

Benchmarks: closed. The MMLU gap between the best open and best proprietary models went from 17.5 points at the end of 2023 to 0.3 points at the end of 2024 (Stanford AI Index), effectively closed since. The Stanford AI Index 2026 puts the capability gap between the top closed and top open models at 3.3% (March 2026: Claude Opus 4.6 at 1,503 against GLM-5 at 1,454). The honest caveat: MMLU is saturating, so the benchmark gap is the weakest measure of the real gap.

Time to frontier: widening. Epoch AI puts the average time lag between open and closed models at about 4 months (ECI, May 2026), up from 3 months in late 2025. The models are closer on capability, and further behind on timing. That is the most important number in this thesis: a gap that closes on benchmarks while widening on time is a gap that is getting harder to close, because the frontier keeps moving.

Hard tasks: the gap persists. On the hardest long-horizon engineering work, the open-to-closed ratio is 0.55. On SWE-bench Pro, one 2026 analysis puts the gap at 18.2 points, against 0.2 points on saturated SWE-bench Verified. The counter-evidence: SWE-bench Pro itself shows GLM-5.2 at 62.1% against Claude Opus 4.8 at 69.2% (Zhipu-reported, June 2026), and FrontierSWE at 74.4% against 75.1%, a near-tie. The gap is real on the hardest tasks and small on the ones that matter most to working developers.

Price: the open advantage. DeepSeek V4 Flash was priced at $0.14 input and $0.28 output per million tokens at its April 2026 launch, against Claude Opus 4.8 at $5.00 and $25.00, about 89x cheaper on output. The current DeepSeek list has moved up ($0.30 and $1.20 peak for V4.1-Flash), which is itself a signal: open models are pricing up as they get better, and the price gap narrows as the capability gap closes. US companies routed more than 30% of OpenRouter tokens to Chinese open models weekly from February 8, 2026, touching 46%: the market is voting with its tokens.

03

The field

The key open model families and their standing. Who is closing, and who is falling behind.

DeepSeek, closing. V4 Pro (April 2026, MIT, 1.6T total parameters, 49B active, 1M context) and V4.1-Flash (October 17, 2026, 552B, MIT). The price leader and the benchmark leader among open models, and the one the closed providers watch most closely.

Kimi and Moonshot, closing. Kimi K3 (July 2026, 2.8T total, 104B active, 1M context, custom license) and Kimi K3.1 Pro (November 2026, weights public by November 5). The largest open models by parameter count, and the ones with the longest context windows.

GLM and Zhipu, closing. GLM-5.2 (June 2026, MIT, 753B total, 40B active, 1M context) and GLM-5.3 (August 18, 2026, custom license, 744B total, 40B active). The closest to the frontier on the hard benchmarks, and the one with the near-tie on FrontierSWE.

Qwen and Alibaba, closing. Qwen3.8-27B (August 14, 2026, Apache 2.0; vendor-reported 15 of 19 benchmarks against Claude Opus 4.6 Max) and Qwen3.8-Max 2.4T (announced August 2, 2026). The most permissively licensed of the large open families, and the one with the broadest ecosystem.

Llama and Meta, falling behind. Llama 4 Scout and Maverick (April 5, 2025, Llama 4 Community License) are falling behind. Meta's frontier line is now closed: Muse Spark (April 8, 2026) is proprietary, API-only, with no downloadable weights, and Llama 4's largest variant Behemoth (2T parameters) was abandoned. Meta released Muse Spark Open on October 15, 2026 (816B total, 42B active, 1M context, open weights) and Muse Glimmer on August 10, 2026 (30B dense multimodal, Apache 2.0). The frontier is closed; the open releases sit a step behind it. Watch whether the gap widens past the point where fine-tuned open models can stay near closed frontier performance: Meta was the largest Western open-weight provider, and its frontier is no longer open.

Mistral, holding. Mistral Large 3 (Apache 2.0, 256K context) and Mistral Large 4 (preview API October 6, 2026, weights promised). The European open-weight provider, steady but not leading.

Beyond language models. Open voice: Whisper large-v3 (OpenAI, MIT, 1.55B parameters, 99 languages, still the default speech recognizer), Nemotron 3.5 Transcribe (Nvidia, October 2026, open weights, ties for first among open-weight speech recognition at a 2.8% word error rate, 40 languages), Kokoro-82M (Apache 2.0, text-to-speech, faster than real time on a CPU), Chatterbox (Resemble AI, MIT, text-to-speech with emotion control), and Bark 3.0 (163MB, CC-BY 4.0). Open image: FLUX.1 (Black Forest Labs; [dev] is the open variant, the quality leader), Stable Diffusion 3.5 (the ecosystem play), and MiniMax H3-Image (November 2026, open weights, 4K images). Open video: Wan 2.2 (Alibaba), Hunyuan Video (Tencent), and Pika H3 Flex (open weights, top on DesignArena's text-to-video board). The open ecosystem is strongest where the closed providers have the least to protect, and weakest at the frontier of language reasoning.

04

Distillation

Many open models are distilled from closed models. The closed providers lobby government and use technical safeguards to kill the open community. The mechanism, honestly, and what evidence exists for it.

Distillation is the open ecosystem's open secret: many open models are trained, in part, on the outputs of closed models, which the closed providers' terms forbid. It is frowned upon, it is widespread, and it is the reason the gap closes as fast as it does: the open models learn from the closed models' answers. The closed providers respond in two ways: lobbying government to restrict open access, and technical safeguards (anti-distillation) to make it harder to learn from their outputs.

The DePue forensic analysis. Will DePue (Unemployment Labs), November 12, 2026, published a "checkpoint forensics" analysis finding Blackwell initializer fingerprints in six models: DeepSeek V4-Flash, V4-Pro, Kimi K3, Kimi K3.1-Pro, MiniMax-M3, and Ant Ling-3.1-flash (148 of 152 streaming multiprocessors). Earlier models read Hopper; the fingerprints were verified on physical B300 and GB200 hardware. What this proves: Blackwell-class hardware generated the starting weights. What it does not prove: that Blackwell did the training, where the training happened, or that any export control was violated. There is no fingerprint in V4.1-Flash, and Qwen reads Hopper. The analysis is self-published, not peer-reviewed, and PyTorch is removing the fingerprinting. Attribute it as reported analysis, not proven fact. Its significance is not legal but structural: the open frontier is built on the same hardware the closed providers buy, and the hardware trail is now visible.

The honest read. Distillation is both the open ecosystem's accelerator and its vulnerability. If the closed providers succeed in making distillation technically impossible or legally punishable, the open models lose their fastest route to the frontier, and the time lag widens further. If they fail, the open models keep closing the gap by learning from the closed models' answers. The distillation fight is the model war in its most concrete form.

05

The regulatory threat to open

The mechanisms by which the closed side could kill open. Tracked as needles.

The BIS interim final rule, November 16, 2026. The Bureau of Industry and Security closed the remote-access loophole: the rule that bites open access to frontier compute. If enforced strictly, it limits who can use frontier hardware remotely, which is how much of the open ecosystem trains.

The four pending bills. The AI Overwatch Act, the Chip Security Act, the MATCH Act, and the Cloud Security Act. Each is a mechanism by which the closed side could cut open access to frontier data or compute: export controls extended to DUV and servicing (MATCH Act), chip security and tracking (Chip Security Act), cloud access controls (Cloud Security Act), and oversight of AI development (AI Overwatch Act). None has passed. All four are live, and any one passing is a needle event.

The regulatory threat is the most concrete way the black hole wins: not by building a better model, but by making it illegal or impossible to build an open one. The closed providers sought government capture to guarantee their monopoly, and it has not worked so far. The bills are the next attempt.

06

The sovereignty leg

Open weights in sovereign environments. Open models are the raw material of the sovereignty defense.

The sovereignty pivot is the counter-move to the black hole: customers who refuse to hand over their alpha run open models in their own environments, where the data stays inside and the model answers to them. Palantir plus Armada sovereign AI (October 7, 2026) and Palantir plus Nvidia Nemotron open weights in Palantir sovereign environments (June 29, 2026) are the two concrete deployments. The mechanism is the ontology layer above the data plane: the customer keeps its own ontology, runs open weights inside it, and the closed provider never sees the alpha. If the sovereignty pivot scales, the funnel starves and the black hole stops growing. If it stalls, the closed providers keep the funnel fed.

07

The point of no return

An open question with the conditions that would settle it. Not declared reached, not declared unreachable.

Have the black holes reached a point of no return? The test that would settle it has two conditions, and both must hold: closed providers achieving durable customer capture, AND open models losing access to frontier data or compute. If only one holds, the question stays open: capture without a data and compute cutoff can be reversed by a sovereignty pivot, and a cutoff without capture leaves customers free to use what open models already exist. If both hold, the black hole has won: the customers are locked in and the alternative is starved. Neither condition is met today. The capture is unproven (the Anthropic and OpenAI theses both find it unproven), and the cutoff is pending (the BIS rule and the four bills are live but not yet biting). The point of no return is a test, not a declaration, and this thesis updates it as the conditions move.

08

Needle milestones

The small set of milestones that move the measured odds of this thesis the most.

  • Gap benchmarks: open matches or passes closed (growth): an open model matches or passes the best closed model on a major benchmark (SWE-bench Pro, Terminal-Bench, or a frontier eval). Down: open models fall behind on the next major benchmark by a widening margin.
  • A major open release matching the frontier (growth): a major open release (DeepSeek, Kimi, GLM, Qwen, or a new entrant) matches frontier capability at release. Down: the next major open release lands months behind the frontier with a wider capability gap.
  • A regulatory kill-shot on open (risk-mitigation): a bill passes or a rule bites that cuts open access to frontier data or compute (BIS rule enforcement, MATCH Act, Chip Security Act, AI Overwatch Act, Cloud Security Act). Up: the bills stall and the BIS rule is narrowed or delayed, leaving open access intact.
  • A sovereign deployment at scale (growth): a sovereign deployment at scale (Palantir plus Armada, or a government-wide open-weights deployment) goes live and expands. Down: sovereign deployments stall or a major customer returns to a closed provider.

09

Verdict: the gap trajectory

Closing on capability, widening on time. The verdict is the trajectory, and it moves as the gap moves.

The benchmark gaps have effectively closed (MMLU 17.5 points to 0.3), the price gap is enormous (about 89x on output), and US companies are routing a growing share of tokens to Chinese open models. But the average time lag grew from 3 months to 4 months, the gap persists on the hardest agentic tasks (a 0.55 ratio on the hardest long-horizon engineering), and Meta, the largest Western open-weight provider, has moved its frontier behind closed doors. The open ecosystem is winning on capability and price while losing on time. That is not a stable trajectory: if the time lag keeps widening, the benchmark gap will follow it. If a major open release matches the frontier at release, the lag closes and the verdict changes. The verdict is updated as the gap moves, and the gap is measured as a live series, not a single snapshot.

01

What: the end goal

Own both parents of Terafab, SpaceX (SPCX, including xAI) and Tesla (TSLA), to capture the maximum share of the value created by their joint AI-chip fab, plus the merger optionality around it.

Contested premise: "both parents" is itself one possible outcome of the operator question, now formally in dispute (see below). Under TSMC’s stated position, the Musk companies could end up anchor customers of a TSMC-owned-and-operated fab rather than its parents. The staged TSLA leg stays the event-driven kicker and the hedge in either outcome.

SPCX is the core compounder. The staged TSLA leg is the event-driven kicker and the hedge. Whichever way the corporate structure resolves, merged, partnered, or separate, the fab economics land in the portfolio.

The operator question: contested as of Oct 21, 2026

The two principals are on record with incompatible positions on who owns and runs Terafab:

  • Musk (Oct 7): SpaceX and Tesla will build and run the fab themselves. "No, we will build and run the fab. ZERO doubt... Maybe TSMC subleases part of the Terafab if they want."
  • TSMC Chairman/CEO C.C. Wei (Oct 15, Q3 earnings call): Terafab talks are real, TSMC teams are in Texas evaluating, and the fab "has got to be run by TSMC," nonnegotiable. Ownership left open: a wholly TSMC-owned facility or a partnership.

This is the gating variable for the thesis: it decides who captures the operating margin and the process expertise, and whether Terafab is a Musk-network asset or a TSMC asset with the Musk companies as anchor customers. Intel CEO Lip-Bu Tan has separately confirmed a Terafab partner role (Oct 9); scope still undefined, and it sits on both sides of the operator binary.

02

Why: the Terafab economics

  • Chips are the binding constraint.SpaceX’s S-1 calls chip production “one of the primary bottlenecks to AI’s continued growth.” Whoever owns the fab owns the margin layer everyone else rents.
  • Demand is internal and already contracted.Colossus (xAI), Starlink/Starshield satellites, Optimus (Musk calls Terafab “critical” to scaling the robot), plus FSD/Cybercab. Externally, the Google AI compute pact ($920M/month for 32 months) proves third parties will buy SpaceX compute.
  • Full-stack control.The pair claims “greater control over the full physical AI stack than any other AI player.” Silicon to orbit to robot, no NVIDIA tax, but the silicon layer is precisely what is now contested. If TSMC owns or operates the fab, part of the NVIDIA tax is replaced by TSMC’s operating margin and process control. (See the operator question above.)
  • Derisking from Taiwan.Terafab is also a break from dependence on Taiwanese foundries, the concentration risk if China moves on Taiwan. Putting AI-chip supply inside U.S. borders is the strategic point. And TSMC is not merely at the table: on Oct 15 Chairman/CEO C.C. Wei confirmed Terafab talks are real, said TSMC teams are in Texas evaluating, and set a nonnegotiable condition: the fab "has got to be run by TSMC." Ownership is open (wholly TSMC-owned or a partnership); the operator question is formally contested between the two principals.
  • Two parents, one fab, zero leakage.Owning both companies means Terafab’s value reaches you however it’s split between them, and the merger, if it happens, simply internalizes it.

03

How: the position

Capital flows to both parents; silicon demand flows to the fab; margin flows back. The dashed hedge means a merger can’t transfer value away from you.

  • SPCX core:hold. Wins on Starlink, launch cadence, and orbital compute with or without a merger.
  • TSLA leg (staged):(1) discounted entry on China-restructuring weakness, (2) merger speculation / target premium, (3) dilution hedge: if SpaceX issues premium-priced shares to buy Tesla, the TSLA leg catches the receiving side, (4) standalone floor: autonomy, robotics, energy, Terafab, and Tesla’s verified ~19M-share SpaceX stake.

Same $X, two ways to own the future

Put the full $X into SpaceX today, or split it into $(X−Y) of SpaceX and $Y of Tesla. Either way the same total capital is invested in the same future: the two companies and their joint Terafab bet. The split exists for one reason: if a merger happens, the TSLA shares collect the takeover premium, offsetting the dilution on the SpaceX side, leaving you with the same, or more, of the combined company. If no merger comes, the full $X is still at work in both names.

The split costs nothing where nothing happens, and pays where the merger does. Assumes the $Y is accumulated pre-announcement at undisturbed prices, the playbook’s no-chase rule. Bar widths proportional to the illustrative share counts.

The empire: every company is a supporting character

No Elon Musk company stands alone, and the Terafab endgame runs through all of them. Each company is a supporting character in the others' stories and a potential future merger. Two combinations are already complete:

  • xAI acquired X, March 28, 2025(verified): all-stock; xAI at $80B, X at $33B. Rationale: combine "the data, models, compute, distribution and talent."
  • SpaceX acquired xAI, February 2, 2026(verified): all-stock; SpaceX at $1T, xAI at $250B; closed May 2026, rebranded SpaceXAI July 2026. SpaceX's ~$8B 2025 profit funds xAI's cash burn.

Terafab itself is an empire project: first reported as a $20-25B chip fabrication joint effort spanning Tesla, SpaceX, and xAI, targeting 2nm process for orbital data centers, but public records exposed in October 2026 (Grimes County notices, reported by ENR, Realty Today, Dealroom, Houston Business Journal, and KBTX) point to a much larger gated buildout: ~$55B for the initial phases, up to $119B across four phases through 2036, with go/no-go decisions per phase (Phase 2 decisions no earlier than the end of the first construction cycle). The demand is internal and contracted across the empire before a single outside customer exists, though who owns and runs the fab is now formally contested (see the operator question above); the empire framing describes the demand and funding side regardless of the outcome.

  • xAI / SpaceXAI (the brain):Grok runs in Tesla vehicles (agentic vehicle control since September 2026); Tesla Megapacks power the Colossus 2 data center (720 containers, 3.3 GWh, the largest grid-connected battery in the US; ~$1B in Megapack sales to the cluster). Tesla's $2B xAI investment converted to a sub-1% SpaceX holding. Joint "Digital Optimus" project confirmed March 2026.
  • X (the data refinery):Grok trains on the real-time corpus of 600M+ users; the empire's broadcast wire. Musk announces milestones there first.
  • Neuralink (the longest-dated call option):26+ implants by mid-2026; $650M Series E at $9B (June 2025), secondary marks near $54-60B; $0 revenue. Musk's stated endgame is Neuralink plus Optimus. Nothing demonstrated yet.
  • The Boring Company (the physical infrastructure arm):$3B Series D at $23B (September 2026, UAE-led). Vegas Loop runs Teslas with FSD trials; the Giga Texas Cybertunnel (online 2025) serves Tesla logistics.

Why this matters for the Terafab position

The chip fab's customers, its power infrastructure (Megapacks), its AI workloads (Grok, Optimus), and its funding (SpaceX profits) all live inside the same empire. Terafab is not a merchant foundry bet; it is vertical integration of the empire's own AI stack, from silicon to orbit. A SpaceX-Tesla combination would put the fab, the robots, and the intelligence under one capitalization table, which is the endgame this document is sized for.
